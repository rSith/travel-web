A small town on the south-west coast

Ambalangoda sits on the Galle Road, with the Indian Ocean on one side and coconut trees on the other. The train runs so close to the sea that you can watch the waves from your seat.

People here are proud of two things: the beach, and the craft of mask making. Think of it like a village that is famous for one special food. Ask anyone in town and they will point you to a mask workshop.

Where
Galle District, about 90 km south of Colombo
Known for
Traditional masks, Ayurveda, sea turtles
Getting there
About 2 hours by train from Colombo
Best months
December to April, when the weather is dry

Things to do

  • Ariyapala Mask Museum

    See old masks on display and learn how they are carved and painted. It is like walking through a picture book of village stories.

  • Sea turtle hatchery

    Visit a place that protects turtle eggs and looks after baby turtles until they are ready to go to the sea.

  • Ambalangoda beach

    Walk on a long, calm beach. Go in the early morning to see fishermen bring in the catch, or stay for the sunset.

  • Madu River boat ride

    A short drive north, take a small boat through mangroves and tiny islands. It feels like a green tunnel on the water.

The masks of Ambalangoda

For a long time, masks made here have been used in dance, drama and healing ceremonies. Makers carve them from light wood, then paint them with bright colours.

Kolam

Used in folk plays that make fun of kings, soldiers and villagers. Like a comedy show, but with wooden faces.

Sanni

Worn in a healing ritual. Each mask stands for a different sickness, so the dancer can "act out" the problem and send it away.

Raksha

Big, fierce demon faces with wide eyes and sharp teeth. They are made to look scary on purpose.

Plan your visit

Ambalangoda is easy to reach and easy to walk around. You do not need a big plan. One day is enough to see the main places, and two days lets you slow down.

  • By train: take the coastal line from Colombo Fort and sit on the left side for sea views.
  • By bus: buses run all day along Galle Road.
  • Around town: tuk-tuks are cheap, so agree on the price before you start.
  • Nearby: Hikkaduwa, Galle Fort and Bentota are all a short ride away.
